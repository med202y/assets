## 📊 Dataset Description, Sources & Data Quality Guidelines

### 1. Dataset Overview

Our project uses a combination of historical financial market data and U.S. macroeconomic indicators to build a unified dataset for financial analysis and modelling.

The dataset is designed to cover the **United States financial market**, using daily observations over a common historical period:

- **Start date:** September 2004
- **End date:** November 4, 2026 (intended extraction endpoint)
- **Frequency:** Daily
- **Geographical scope:** United States
- **Reference time zone:** America/New_York
- **Currency:** USD for ETF prices

September 2004 is selected as the starting point because VNQ, the last ETF in our selection to begin trading, started in September 2004.

All variables are aligned using their observation dates to create a common dataset.

### 2. Data Sources and Origins

We rely on two primary data providers:

#### A. Yahoo Finance — Financial Market Data

Source: https://finance.yahoo.com/

Yahoo Finance is used to retrieve historical adjusted closing prices for four exchange-traded funds (ETFs).

| Ticker | Instrument | Represents |
|---|---|---|
| SPY | SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust | U.S. equity market |
| IEF | iShares 7–10 Year Treasury Bond ETF | U.S. government bonds |
| LQD | iShares iBoxx Investment Grade Corporate Bond ETF | Investment-grade corporate bonds |
| VNQ | Vanguard Real Estate ETF | U.S. real estate market |

**Why adjusted closing prices?**

Adjusted closing prices account for corporate actions such as stock splits and distributions. They are more appropriate than ordinary closing prices when studying historical investment performance and calculating returns.

#### B. FRED — Macroeconomic and Financial Indicators

Source: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/

FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data), maintained by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, provides the macroeconomic and financial indicators.

| Variable | FRED Series ID | Description |
|---|---|---|
| VIX | VIXCLS | CBOE Volatility Index |
| BAA10Y | BAA10Y | Moody's Baa corporate bond yield relative to the 10-year Treasury yield |
| DGS3MO | DGS3MO | 3-month U.S. Treasury constant maturity rate |
| DGS2 | DGS2 | 2-year U.S. Treasury constant maturity rate |
| DGS10 | DGS10 | 10-year U.S. Treasury constant maturity rate |
| T10Y2Y | T10Y2Y | Difference between 10-year and 2-year Treasury yields |

These indicators provide information about market volatility, credit risk, interest rates and the yield curve.

### 3. Dataset Construction and Temporal Alignment

The datasets are downloaded separately using Python:

- `yfinance` for ETF historical prices.
- FRED CSV endpoints for macroeconomic indicators.
- `pandas` for cleaning, date alignment and merging.

The final dataset is constructed by joining all variables using their observation dates.

An **inner join** is currently used, meaning that only dates available across all selected variables are retained.

The resulting dataset contains:

- 4 ETF price variables.
- 6 macroeconomic and financial indicators.
- One common date index.

All observations are date-based. Yahoo Finance timestamps should be normalized to the U.S. Eastern calendar date before merging with FRED observations.

**Important:** FRED observations are daily date-indexed values, not intraday timestamps. A common date does not guarantee that every indicator was publicly available at the same time.

### 4. Important Points to Keep in Mind

Before using the dataset for analysis or machine learning, every team member should consider the following:

**A. Missing observations**

- Financial markets are closed on weekends and public holidays.
- Some FRED indicators may not be published every business day.
- Missing values must be identified and investigated.
- Do not automatically replace missing values with zero.
- Do not blindly forward-fill observations without considering their meaning.

**B. Historical coverage**

- Although September 2004 is our intended starting point, individual variables may have different historical coverage.
- BAA10Y requires particular attention because its FRED page may indicate that the series has been discontinued.
- Verify the actual last available observation for every variable before accepting the final extraction endpoint.
- Never assume that a requested download date means the data actually exists for that date.

**C. Time zones and publication timing**

- Use America/New_York as the reference time zone for market dates.
- FRED series are date-based and do not necessarily provide publication timestamps.
- An observation's reference date may differ from its actual publication date.
- For predictive modelling, avoid using information that would not have been publicly available at the prediction time.

**D. Data consistency and units**

- ETF adjusted prices are expressed in USD.
- Treasury yields and credit spreads are expressed in percentage points.
- VIX is an index, not a percentage interest rate.
- T10Y2Y represents a yield difference, not an independent interest rate.
- Do not combine these variables without considering their different scales and financial meanings.

**E. Financial data and look-ahead bias**

If the dataset is used for forecasting, portfolio optimization or investment strategy evaluation:

- Calculate returns from adjusted prices rather than treating price levels as returns.
- Respect chronological ordering when splitting training, validation and testing sets.
- Avoid random train/test splitting for time-series forecasting.
- Account for publication delays of macroeconomic indicators.
- Avoid using future information when constructing features.

### 5. Recommended Validation Checklist

Before beginning modelling, verify the following:

- [ ] All 10 variables have been successfully downloaded.
- [ ] The actual historical coverage of each series has been checked.
- [ ] The BAA10Y discontinuation status has been investigated.
- [ ] All dates use a consistent observation-date convention.
- [ ] Duplicate dates have been checked.
- [ ] Missing observations have been quantified.
- [ ] Variable units have been verified.
- [ ] No missing values have been silently replaced.
- [ ] The final dataset's first and last dates have been confirmed.
- [ ] Data sources and retrieval dates have been documented.

### 6. Reproducibility and Data Organization

The project should preserve both the original downloaded files and the cleaned dataset.

Recommended structure:

```text
financial_dataset/
│
├── raw/
│   ├── SPY_raw.csv
│   ├── IEF_raw.csv
│   ├── LQD_raw.csv
│   ├── VNQ_raw.csv
│   ├── VIX_raw.csv
│   ├── BAA10Y_raw.csv
│   ├── DGS3MO_raw.csv
│   ├── DGS2_raw.csv
│   ├── DGS10_raw.csv
│   └── T10Y2Y_raw.csv
│
├── processed/
│   └── final_dataset.csv
│
└── download_datasets.py
```

Keep the raw files unchanged so that data cleaning and preprocessing can be reproduced.

**Data usage and attribution:** Yahoo Finance and FRED/Cboe are the respective data origins. Cite the original providers in reports, presentations and research outputs. Check the applicable usage and redistribution conditions before publishing or sharing the downloaded data.

---

**Final note:** This dataset is intended to provide a consistent historical view of U.S. financial markets and macroeconomic conditions. Its reliability depends not only on the sources but also on correct temporal alignment, missing-data treatment and avoiding look-ahead bias.
